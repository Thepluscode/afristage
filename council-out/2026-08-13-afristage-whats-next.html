⛔ Verdict corrected — the kill-condition fired

This council shipped recommending “sell goods, not coins.” The gating check it set for Monday morning was then run against the code, and refuted it in 30 minutes.

The marketplace is priced and settled in coins. The buyer journey is card → coins → goods, never card → goods. So commerce-first would mean two weeks of building before the premise test — precisely the failure this council convened to prevent.

Gating check — answered from the code

How are products priced? In coins. Product.priceCoins, Order.unitPriceCoins / totalCoins / sellerNetCoins / platformFeeCoins.

Does an order touch a payment provider? No. Zero matches for stripe / paystack / checkout / intent across src/modules/marketplace/.

What does a sale move? An internal ledger transfer — MoneyService.purchase() debits the buyer's COIN account, credits sellerEarning + platformRevenue.

Does validate:marketplace prove a cash-out? No. It asserts a creator EARNING delta and stops. No payout leg.

Two findings beyond the question asked — the coins-first architecture IS the IAP exposure (every route to revenue passes through a digital-goods purchase), and the seller cash-out leg is unproven: only creator payouts are covered, so whether a shop owner can withdraw is untested. “Can I get my money out” is the first question any merchant asks.

What all five missed

Taking real money is the one experiment that creates obligations — and all five priced it as free. Every advisor treats “take one real payment” as the cheapest falsifiable check. Legally it is the most expensive one on the list.

  • You hold a float. Coin balances and creator earnings are stored value. Custody + payout is regulated activity (CBN in Nigeria), and Paystack's own terms restrict running a wallet on top of them. This question arrives on the same day as the first naira.
  • The reversibility asymmetry is the classic killer of gifting platforms. Cards reverse for months; payouts don't. Stolen card → coins → gift to accomplice → withdraw before the chargeback. The double-entry ledger records that attack perfectly and stops none of it.
  • A stated payout hold (T+14) mitigates both, and costs nothing — it preserves the entire premise signal while removing the float exposure and the chargeback window.

Nobody asked about runway either. The Premise Gate governs when to stop spending; no advisor asked how many months of spending remain.

The five advisors

Outsider(d) · on the rail customers actually have

“1020 tests and zero dollars.” Every fact offered as progress is a fact about the inside of the system — correlation IDs, constraints, integrity sweeps. Not one is a fact about a person. Thirteen ledger transactions is not a beta, it's a demo.

On the record: the money loop is not verified until money has moved on Paystack from a real African card into a real creator's balance and out again.

Executor(d) · starts clocks you don't control

The only option whose first action starts a clock the founder doesn't own. Paystack hands over test keys on signup; live keys wait on verification. Option (c) is a 30-minute paperwork errand, not a 2–4 week investment.

On the record: background jobs is the only option with zero founder-gated latency — and that is not a point in its favour. It will feel productive while every clock that matters stays unstarted.

First-Principlesreframe · creator recruitment

The unit of value isn't a feature or a user — it's a creator who can drag an existing audience across a platform switch. The real premise is narrower than “someone pays”: will a creator move her audience here, and will it pay here? Testable in a week with zero code.

On the record: the verified money loop is verified in the wrong currency, on the wrong rail, for the wrong customer.

Contrarian(d) · least-fatal failure mode

(a) fails by being correct — success indistinguishable from doing nothing, on the fourth consecutive hardening cycle. (b) spends an asset you cannot re-buy: a creator gets one first impression, and an empty room ends it.

On the record: Apple and Google require in-app purchase for digital goods, and gifting coins are digital goods — 30% off the top, and an in-app Stripe sheet is grounds for rejection. Every unit economic implied by the verified loop is wrong for the channel you're building toward.

Expansionist(d) enlarged · first live sale

The coin economy is the small business you happened to build first. The marketplace is commerce — real goods, real margin, money already in the buyer's budget. Stripe-verified-and-Paystack-not is a map, not a blocker: it points at the diaspora, where ARPU is many times higher.

On the record: run the premise test as “will viewers buy coins” and a failure reads as “nobody pays for AfriStage” — a false negative that kills a live-commerce payments company because its weakest revenue line underperformed.

The reinforcement neither advisor saw — real, but latent

IAP and the marketplace could solve each other. The Contrarian's sharpest point is that gifting coins are digital goods — 30% to Apple, and an in-app card sheet risks rejection. The Expansionist's sharpest point is that the marketplace sells physical goods — which are exempt from the IAP rule, as every commerce app demonstrates.

⛔ Corrected by the gating check. The exemption requires the buyer to pay for goods with a card. AfriStage's marketplace is priced in coins and settled by internal ledger transfer, so the card still buys a digital good. The combination is real but latent — true only once a direct card-checkout path for physical orders exists.

The durable half: coins are the revenue line most likely to be taxed 30% at exactly the moment distribution is solved, and the marketplace is the lane where that tax can legally be avoided — once it takes card payments directly.
